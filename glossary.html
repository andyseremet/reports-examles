What the words mean

Short, plain meanings of the words used in the report, in A to Z order.

Air quality management area (AQMA)
An area where the council has found that air pollution, usually from traffic, is above the national limits. The council must have a plan to bring it down.
Ancient woodland
Woodland that has existed since at least 1600 in England and Wales (1750 in Scotland). It is protected and cannot be replaced, so building near it is restricted.
Brownfield land
Land that has been built on before, such as old factories or yards. Councils list it as land that could be used for new homes, so it may be built on in future.
Coal mining report (CON29M)
A report from the Mining Remediation Authority (formerly the Coal Authority) on past and present coal mining under and near a property. Your conveyancer orders it in coal mining areas.
Coal tip
A heap of waste rock left by coal mining. In Wales the government keeps a list of disused tips and checks the ones that could slip.
COMAH site (major hazard site)
A site that stores large amounts of dangerous substances, such as fuel or chemicals. COMAH stands for Control of Major Accident Hazards, the rules these sites must follow.
Conservation area
An area the council protects for its special character. Changes such as new windows, extensions or cutting down trees may need permission.
Contaminated land
Land that a council has officially found to be polluted enough to cause harm and that must be cleaned up. Few sites are listed, so an environmental search is still worth having.
Conveyancer
The solicitor or licensed conveyancer who handles the legal side of buying a home, including the searches.
Data zone
A small area of Scotland, usually 500 to 1,000 people, used for official statistics such as crime and deprivation.
Deprivation index
An official ranking of small areas by income, jobs, health, education, crime, housing and services. Each UK country has its own (England's IMD, Wales's WIMD, Scotland's SIMD and Northern Ireland's NIMDM).
Environmental search
A report a conveyancer buys about flooding, pollution, old landfill and ground problems at a property. It goes further than this free check.
Flood defence
A wall, bank or barrier built to hold back a river or the sea. Defences lower the chance of flooding but do not remove it.
Flood zones 1, 2 and 3
Official flood risk areas for rivers and the sea, as if there were no flood defences. Zone 1 is low (less than a 1 in 1,000 chance each year), Zone 2 medium (1 in 1,000 to 1 in 100 from rivers), and Zone 3 high (1 in 100 or more from rivers, 1 in 200 or more from the sea).
Green Belt
Open land around towns and cities where new building is strongly restricted, to stop towns spreading.
Groundwater flooding
Flooding when water under the ground rises above the surface, often into cellars, after long wet spells.
HS2 safeguarding
Land kept free from other building because it may be needed for the HS2 railway. Homes on it may be bought by the government.
Historic landfill
A place where waste was buried in the past. It can give off gas or affect the ground, so it is worth asking about if one is close.
Landslide
Ground, rock or soil moving down a slope. The British Geological Survey records past landslides.
Lden (noise level)
The average noise level over 24 hours, in decibels (dB), with evening and night noise counted as louder. The World Health Organization recommends road noise below 53 dB.
Listed building
A building protected for its history or design, graded I, II* or II. Most changes to it need listed building consent from the council.
Local search
Questions your conveyancer asks the council (forms CON29 and LLC1) about planning, roads, notices and charges on a property.
Median
The middle value when all values are put in order. Half the sales were above it and half below, so one very high or low price does not change it much.
National Landscape
In England and Wales, the new name for an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty (AONB): countryside protected for its beauty, where building is limited. National Parks are protected in a similar way.
Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂)
A gas that comes mostly from vehicle exhausts. High levels can harm breathing, especially near busy roads.
PM2.5
Very fine dust in the air, smaller than 2.5 thousandths of a millimetre, from traffic, wood burning and industry. It can get deep into the lungs.
Radon
A natural radioactive gas from the ground that can build up indoors. A radon test is cheap, and high levels can be fixed, often with a small fan under the floor.
Scheduled monument
A nationally important archaeological site or historic structure, such as a burial mound or castle ruin. Work on or near it needs permission.
Shoreline management plan
A long-term plan for a stretch of coast that says whether its defences will be kept, moved back, or left to the sea.
Shrink-swell clay
Clay ground that shrinks in dry weather and swells when wet. It can move foundations and crack walls, especially near trees.
Special Area of Conservation and Special Protection Area
The highest protection for wildlife habitats and birds. Building nearby may need extra checks.
Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI)
Land protected for its wildlife, plants or rocks. Work on or near it may need permission from the nature agency.
Surface water flooding
Flooding when heavy rain cannot drain away fast enough and runs across the ground. It can happen far from any river.
